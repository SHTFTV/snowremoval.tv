Vancouver, are your winter tires ready? A snowfall preparedness checklist.

SnowRemoval.TV editorial · Published October 30, 2026

An Edmonton snowfall is a timely prompt for Vancouver: check the tires for your actual route, leave more travel time, and connect the driveway to a clear walking route.

Original Vancouver adaptation. Source reporting is from Edmonton on October 28; the checklist below is local preparedness analysis, not a Vancouver snowfall alert.

Edmonton’s snowfall is a prompt—not a Vancouver forecast

CityNews carried a Canadian Press report on October 28, 2026 describing Edmonton’s first significant snowfall of the season. The report warned of travel disruption and changing conditions. The CityNews video linked below also examines collisions and plowing questions. Neither item establishes snow in Vancouver today. The useful local lesson is to make decisions before a difficult morning: vehicle readiness, the journey itself and the route from the parked car to the door need one connected plan.

1. Choose for the roads you actually use

ICBC recommends four matching mountain/snowflake winter tires for drivers who regularly encounter snow, ice or slush. Discuss your usual conditions with a tire retailer, including trips beyond Vancouver. Ask about the condition and suitability of the complete set, rather than treating the presence of a symbol as a fresh inspection. A vehicle used only near home and one regularly driven toward higher-elevation destinations may need different preparation conversations.

2. Check condition before the rush

Have tread and condition assessed, and check cold tire pressure against the vehicle manufacturer’s specification. ICBC notes that the maximum pressure printed on a tire is not necessarily the vehicle’s correct inflation pressure. Keep the vehicle manual available and raise any damage or uncertainty with a qualified tire professional. This checklist does not select a tire, diagnose damage or replace an inspection. Book any needed work before an urgent trip leaves little room to change plans.

3. Look up the whole route—not a city-wide assumption

Provincial winter-tire requirements apply to designated routes and posted periods. Do not read this article as saying that every Vancouver street has the same legal tire mandate. Check the Province’s current route information and DriveBC for the journey you intend to take, including return travel. Make a separate decision about whether the trip should proceed. A familiar destination does not guarantee familiar conditions along the way.

4. Allow time, reduce speed and leave room

ICBC’s winter-driving guidance emphasizes preparation and slower travel. Leave more following space and avoid building a schedule around normal dry-road travel times. Tell anyone expecting you that changing conditions may delay or cancel the trip. Clearing your own driveway does not establish that surrounding streets, ramps or the destination are ready. If conditions exceed your ability or equipment, choose a safer alternative rather than trying to make up lost time.

5. Make the last few metres part of the plan

A ready vehicle can still be stranded behind a blocked gate or leave someone stepping onto an untreated path. Before snowfall, identify the parking position, the walking route to the entrance and any accessible route that must remain connected. Move loose objects out of the clearing area and discuss where snow can be placed. Keep drains and sightlines in that conversation. Do not direct cleared snow onto a public travel route or assume the next team will finish the boundary.

6. Agree who checks access after clearing

For a shared property, name the person who checks the entrance, the person who communicates with the provider and the contact who can authorize a follow-up visit. Ask how plowing, hand clearing and ice treatment fit together in the quoted scope. A municipal road pass and a private driveway visit are different responsibilities; a fresh bank at an entrance may need attention. Record what was observed and when. No service visit guarantees that a surface stays free of ice as conditions change.
