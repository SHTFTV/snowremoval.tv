Richmond Snow and Ice Checklist for Strata Owners and Councils

A practical Richmond strata checklist for sidewalks, entrance paths, approved ice-control materials, refreezing checks and resident reporting.

Make the winter plan useful to the person walking it

For a resident leaving early, the important details are the path from the entrance, the stairs, the accessible route and the sidewalk outside. Start your Richmond strata winter review with those journeys. Take a site walk, list the routes and check the current service agreement against them. The goal is a plan residents can understand and the responsible people can act on when conditions change.

Identify the adjacent sidewalk frontage

Richmond requires snow and ice cleared from adjacent sidewalks by 10 a.m. every day, Sundays included. Locate the frontage along the property line and confirm who performs the work. Keep that instruction visible in the service brief and handover notes. Ask how completion and any remaining concern will be reported. Do not assume that a general parking-lot instruction answers the separate question of sidewalk clearing.

Check every priority entrance

List the main door, accessible entrance, stairs, ramps, mail or parcel access and the walking route from parking. Mark areas where residents are likely to walk when another route is blocked. Use consistent names on the map and in resident notices. Ask the provider which areas need hand work and how they fit the visit. If a route is temporarily restricted, communicate the appropriate alternate access through the building’s normal channel.

Know your surfaces before approving a product

Record asphalt, mature concrete, new concrete, pavers and decorative finishes. Collect installer and sealer care instructions where treatment restrictions may apply. Ask for the product name, operating instructions and intended application areas before materials are ordered. Keep the labels available to staff or volunteers handling any part of the property. A product that works on one driving lane should not automatically become the approved treatment for every entrance pad.

Sand is used locally, but it solves a different problem

Richmond describes using sand in its road-treatment mix. That establishes local use, not a blanket instruction for your property. Sand adds traction rather than melting ice. Ask the contractor to explain why the chosen material suits the surface, conditions and maintenance routine. If traction material is used, include inspection, replenishment and later cleanup in the plan. Keep the decision tied to your property instructions rather than a general road policy.

Include checks when conditions change

Identify downspouts, low spots, shade and places where meltwater can cross a path. Ask who reviews those areas after clearing, rainfall or a thaw followed by freezing. Confirm how a return visit is requested and whether it is included in the service scope. If a new concern appears, report the location and time promptly. A visit record should distinguish an inspection, an application and an issue requiring more attention.

Give residents a simple reporting process

Publish one contact route for ordinary snow and ice concerns, with a backup for the building team. Ask residents for the location, time and description; photographs are helpful only when they can be taken safely. Keep the status clear: reported, acknowledged, scheduled or addressed. The City advises keeping cleared snow off the street. Agree private storage locations in advance and keep pedestrian paths, drains and access points clear of stored piles.

Your ready-to-use checklist

Confirm sidewalk responsibility and the morning schedule. Map entrances and accessible routes. Collect surface-care instructions. Agree materials, dispatch triggers and return checks. Choose storage areas and identify drainage concerns. Nominate main and backup contacts. Set the resident reporting route and visit record. Frequently asked questions

Who clears the sidewalk outside a Richmond strata?

The owner or occupier is responsible for the adjacent sidewalk frontage, with snow and ice removed by 10 a.m. daily, Sundays included.

Does a cleared City road complete the strata’s snow service?

No. Confirm the private walking routes, entrances, vehicle areas and ice-control checks in the property service agreement.
