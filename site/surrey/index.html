Snow Removal Regulations

In Surrey, snow removal is governed by specific bylaws that ensure public safety and accessibility during winter months. Property owners are required to clear snow and ice from sidewalks adjacent to their properties within 24 hours of a snowfall. Contractors must adhere to these timelines and ensure that snow is not piled onto public roads or obstructing access to fire hydrants. Compliance with these regulations is crucial to avoid fines and ensure the community remains safe and accessible. Understanding these bylaws helps contractors and property managers maintain high standards of service while operating within legal requirements.

Property Owner Responsibilities

  • Shovel snow onto your property, not the street

  • Clear storm drains and grates
  • Maintain clear pathways for pedestrians

Surrey Snow Removal Bylaws & Penalties

The City of Surrey enforces strict snow removal bylaws under the Property Maintenance and Standards Bylaw (Bylaw No. 7533). Understanding these regulations is essential for all property owners.

Key Bylaw Requirements

  • Sidewalk Clearing: Must be cleared by 10 a.m. daily during winter
  • Snow Placement: Snow must be placed on your property only, not on street or neighbor's property
  • Seasonal Duration: Requirements apply whenever snow or ice is present
  • Accessibility: Clear pathways must be safe for pedestrians and wheelchair users
  • Street Parking: Do not block storm drains or city access with snow piles

Fines & Penalties for Non-Compliance

Violation Fine Amount
Snow/ice on sidewalk after 10 a.m. $250
Snow remains over 24 hours $500
Snow blocks street or storm drain $500
Repeat violations (3+ times) Up to $1,000+
Dangerous condition created $1,000-$2,000

Enforcement & City Actions

If property owners fail to comply with bylaws, the City of Surrey may:

  • Issue violation notices with enforcement timelines
  • Perform snow removal at owner's expense (recovery through city billing)
  • Levy ongoing fines for continued violations
  • Place liens on properties for unpaid snow removal costs
  • Report violations to insurance companies

Report Snow & Ice Issues to Surrey 311

Use Surrey's 311 service to report uncleared sidewalks, hazardous icy conditions, and blocked drains on public property. This helps keep neighborhoods safe and ensures code enforcement.

How to Report

Call Surrey 311

Phone: 604-594-6000
Hours: Available during business hours; emergency line 24/7
Cost: Free service for Surrey residents

Online Reporting

Report online through the City of Surrey website:

City of Surrey Website

What to Report

  • Uncleared sidewalks in front of homes or businesses
  • Hazardous icy conditions on public streets
  • Blocked or frozen storm drains
  • Snow piles blocking visibility or traffic flow
  • Inadequate city snow removal on priority routes
  • Dangerous ice on wheelchair accessible paths

Response & Enforcement

Surrey's bylaw enforcement team will:

  • Investigate reported violations within 24-48 hours (weather permitting)
  • Issue violation notices to non-compliant property owners
  • Allow 24 hours for property owners to clear snow
  • Perform city snow removal at owner's expense if non-compliance continues

Surrey School Closures Due to Winter Weather

Winter weather can occasionally force school closures in Surrey. Here's how to stay informed about closures for Surrey's main school districts.

Surrey School District (SD 36)

Decision Time: Closure decisions are made by 6:30 a.m. on the day in question.
  • Largest school district serving Surrey
  • Covers approximately 76,000 students
  • Serves most of Surrey including Whalley, Guildford, Fleetwood areas

How to Get Notifications:

  • Check SD 36 website: www.surreyschools.ca
  • School closure page: SD 36 Closure Information
  • Enable ParentAlert text/email notifications
  • Follow on social media: @SurreySchools
  • Check local news stations (CityNews, CTV, Global)

Langley School District (SD 35) - Southeast Surrey

  • Serves parts of southeast Surrey and Cloverdale
  • Closure decisions by 6:30 a.m.
  • Website: www.sd35.bc.ca
  • Closures page: Weather Closure Information

Winter Closure Preparedness Tips

  • Update emergency contact information with your school immediately
  • Sign up for ParentAlert and all notification systems
  • Don't assume schools are open (announcements only for closures/delays)
  • Have a backup childcare plan ready
  • Check closure announcements by 7 a.m. on winter mornings
  • Monitor weather forecasts the night before

Whalley, Guildford & Fleetwood Schools

Most Surrey schools including those in Whalley, Guildford, and Fleetwood neighborhoods are served by Surrey School District (SD 36). Check the main district website for specific school information and closure announcements.

Surrey 10-Year Snow & Ice Climate Averages

Surrey experiences more significant winter weather than coastal Vancouver due to its inland location and higher elevation. Understanding typical conditions helps you prepare effectively.

Annual Snow Statistics

Metric Average
Annual snowfall 70-90 cm (28-35 inches)
Snow days per year 15-22 days with measurable snow
Days with snow on ground 35-55 days with 1cm+ accumulation
Heaviest month January (4-5 snow days, 150-180mm)
Second heaviest February (3-4 snow days)

Monthly Snow Breakdown

  • December: Variable, 1-3 snow days (30-50mm)
  • January: Heavy snow season (4-5 snow days, 150-180mm average)
  • February: Significant snow (3-4 snow days, 100-130mm)
  • March: Transition month (1-2 snow days, melting occurs)
  • April: Minimal snow (trace to 10mm)
  • May-November: Essentially no snow

Freezing & Ice Days

90-110 days

Average number of days with freezing temperatures annually

Regional Variations Within Surrey
