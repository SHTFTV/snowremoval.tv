Hand Over the Winter Plan Without Losing Site Knowledge

Published October 18, 2026 · PlowWow editorial

Keep one current plan

A replacement manager needs a clear route map, the agreed scope, provider contacts and an issue log. Put the latest versions together and identify who maintains them. Avoid leaving essential gate or access information only in one person’s messages. Share sensitive access details through the property’s approved private channel.

Original PlowWow illustrative brand animation. Not a record of a customer job.

Explain the exceptions

Highlight hand-clearing areas, fragile surfaces, drainage concerns and routes that change when vehicles are parked. Include dated photographs where they explain a condition better than text. Separate confirmed instructions from suggestions that still need the provider’s agreement.

Original PlowWow illustrative brand animation. Not a record of a customer job.

Practise a normal event

Walk through who checks the forecast, initiates work, receives the visit record and inspects afterwards. Confirm the backup contact when someone is away. Ask the incoming person to explain the process back in their own words; missing steps often become apparent during this simple review.

Original PlowWow illustrative brand animation. Not a record of a customer job.

Review after the first handover

Check that the contacts worked and the records were accessible. Update the plan when circumstances change and retire superseded copies from daily use. Keep historical records according to the property’s requirements. The goal is continuity of practical instructions, not a larger collection of unread documents.
