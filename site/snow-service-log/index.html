Record facts you can support

A useful entry identifies the property area, observation time, conditions seen and work reported. Distinguish your own inspection from the contractor’s service record. Do not label a route safe merely because a visit appears on a schedule. Conditions can change between service and inspection.

Original PlowWow illustrative brand animation. Not a record of a customer job.

Use clear photographs

Take a wide view that identifies the area, then a closer view of any issue. Avoid faces, number plates and private interiors where they are unnecessary. Keep original records in the property’s controlled system rather than posting them publicly. An annotated copy can help explain a request to the provider.

Close the communication loop

Note who received the issue, when they acknowledged it and the action agreed. A vague message that says the site is icy may not identify the entrance or urgency. Describe the exact location using the same names as the route plan. Record when the follow-up inspection occurred.

Review recurring problems

Look for repeated blocked gates, drainage trouble and missed hand-clearing areas. Discuss patterns during a calm review rather than waiting for the next storm. A log supports coordination; it is not a substitute for the inspections, advice or records required by your property’s own procedures.
