Strip-Mall Snow Removal in the Lower Mainland: Plan Beyond the Parking Stalls

Published November 11, 2026 · snowremoval.tv editorial

PlowWow’s new rear-plow announcement is a prompt for retail property managers to map customer access, deliveries, snow storage and the full winter-service scope.

A strip mall’s winter plan has to join the parking lot to the doors people use. PlowWow’s November 11 fleet announcement provides a timely reason to review that connection: the operator reported that a BOSS DRAG PRO 8–16 Hydraulic Folding Wing rear plow, RPL21616, was scheduled to go onto one of its trucks that day, and invited enquiries from large commercial lots and strip malls.

For property managers in the Lower Mainland, the useful question is how a proposed equipment setup fits the whole property. SnowRemoval.tv provides planning information; the equipment belongs to PlowWow’s reported fleet addition. Installation completion, route availability and operating results are not established by the announcement.

Map five areas before requesting a commercial snow-removal quote

Use one annotated site plan for customer parking, storefront approaches, accessible access, deliveries and snow storage. Mark the boundary between private property and public space so the provider can explain exactly which areas are included. If the property is in Vancouver, Burnaby, Surrey or another Lower Mainland municipality, give its exact address; a regional description does not settle site access, service coverage or responsibilities.

  • Parking: identify overnight vehicles, islands, dead ends and drive aisles that need to remain usable.
  • Storefronts: identify each tenant entrance and where pulled snow must be moved after it leaves the building edge.
  • Accessible route: connect the parking or drop-off point to the appropriate entrance, including ramps and transitions.
  • Deliveries: show loading doors, vehicle movements and times when clearing equipment needs a separate work window.
  • Storage: identify possible piles and where meltwater may travel, without blocking drains, visibility or pedestrian circulation.

Ask where the rear plow fits, and where other work begins

The BOSS exact-model page lists an eight-to-sixteen-foot rear-mounted plow. That makes open pavement and access geometry relevant to the assessment. It does not establish that a truck can work between every island, bollard or parked vehicle on your site. Ask the provider to explain its clearing sequence and where other equipment or hand work will take over.

A broad parking-lot pass does not clear a staircase, finish every doorway or control ice. List those tasks individually. Ask whether salting or other ice-control treatment, inspections and return visits are part of the proposal. If space for piles runs out, confirm how relocation or hauling would be assessed and authorized before assuming it is included in routine plowing.

Coordinate retail activity with the clearing window

For a strip mall serving several tenants, nominate one person who can approve changes and share access instructions. Gather opening times, delivery windows and any vehicles that cannot move. A workable plan identifies who opens a gate, who communicates with tenants and who tells the provider about a blocked aisle.

Do not promise tenants a finish time based only on the lot’s area or a sixteen-foot blade. The work includes manoeuvring, snow placement and any separately agreed entrances or pedestrian areas. Ask how ongoing snowfall, blocked access and changing conditions affect the agreed service. Keep that answer with the written quote so everyone is referring to the same scope.

Keep a record after each visit

Record the time, the areas observed and any issues still requiring attention. Separate your own inspection from the provider’s report. A clear parking lane and an icy entrance can exist on the same property; report the specific location instead of describing the entire site as complete. Revisit storage and drainage if meltwater crosses a walking route.

The new equipment is business news, not a site-specific performance guarantee. BOSS describes efficiency benefits for rear plows, but no verified average time saving for PlowWow’s new addition is provided here. Use an assessment to agree the work rather than treating the equipment announcement as a service commitment.

Prepare a useful Lower Mainland property enquiry

Send PlowWow the address, approximate paved clearing area, a marked plan or photographs, tenant and delivery schedules, pedestrian priorities and storage options. Ask the operator to confirm coverage, availability, service triggers, included areas and extra-work approval arrangements for that address.

Read the original announcement and request a parking-lot assessment from PlowWow. Its invitation is “Are you ready for Plow Wow?” An enquiry does not reserve a route or confirm a contract.
