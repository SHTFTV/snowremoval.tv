Getting Your Driveway and Walkways Ready Before Snow Arrives

Published September 27, 2026 · SnowRemoval.tv

A practical pre-snow checklist for Lower Mainland homeowners: clear clutter, plan parking, identify walkway priorities and prepare useful enquiry details.

Ask PlowWowRead the guides

Snow Resources · September 27, 2026

It is easier to explain what needs clearing when you can still see the driveway. Before snow arrives, take a short walk around your property and make a plan for vehicle access, foot traffic and the areas you want a provider to review.

This checklist is for Lower Mainland homeowners preparing for a snow-removal enquiry or an upcoming visit. It helps you describe the job clearly without assuming that every driveway, walkway or extra task is included.

Clear the small things first

Move toys, loose hoses, portable planters and other movable items out of the proposed clearing area. Look for objects near steps and along the edge of the driveway that may be difficult to see once covered. Keep bins and stored equipment away from the route you want to use.

Do not move fixed utilities or attempt unfamiliar repairs as part of this preparation. Instead, identify features the provider should know about and discuss any access concerns before work is booked.

Choose the routes that matter most

Make a short priority list: the front entrance, a side entrance used daily, the route to the vehicle, or access to a shared walkway. Describe steps, changes in surface and narrow sections. If there is a shared driveway, coordinate with the other households so everyone understands the proposed scope.

Ask whether the quoted work covers the full driveway, only the accessible area around parked vehicles, walkways, steps or a combination. Separate requests make it easier to compare quotes.

Plan where vehicles will be

Ask how parking affects the visit and whether vehicles need to be moved. Agree on a practical arrangement and provide a contact who can respond when needed. Tell the provider about a low garage entrance, tight turning area or other restriction before equipment arrives.

If you cannot move a vehicle, say so in advance. Confirm what can be reached and what will remain outside the clearing area.

Discuss snow placement and drainage

Identify possible snow-storage areas for the provider to assess. Explain where people walk, where vehicles enter and where water normally drains. Ask what happens if there is not enough room for additional snow and whether any off-site hauling would require a separate quote.

Also discuss whether ice-management work is part of the request. Snow clearing and follow-up surface treatment are different tasks; ask what is included and how additional visits are arranged.

Take useful photos before you enquire

A few current photos can help start the conversation. Include the driveway from the street, the main walkway, steps and any narrow access point. Add approximate dimensions if you have them, and explain the surfaces and priority areas in plain language.

  • Your location and preferred contact details.
  • The driveway and walkway areas you want reviewed.
  • Parking restrictions or shared-access arrangements.
  • Photos showing the layout and visible obstacles.
  • Questions about visit timing, scope and follow-up work.
