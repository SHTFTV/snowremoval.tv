Everything You Need to Know About Snow Removal in Abbotsford

Abbotsford, with a population of 160,000+, is the largest city in the Fraser Valley. Located east of Langley and bordering Mission and the U.S. border at Sumas, Abbotsford experiences colder, snowier winters than coastal Metro Vancouver due to its inland valley position. This comprehensive guide covers Abbotsford's snow removal policies, service priorities, bylaws and penalties, how to report snow issues, school closure procedures, and climate data to help you prepare for winter.

  • Remove ice and apply sand or salt for traction
  • Clear storm drains and grates
  • Maintain clear pathways for pedestrians

Abbotsford Snow Removal Bylaws & Penalties

The City of Abbotsford enforces strict snow removal bylaws under the Good Neighbour Bylaw No. 1256-2003. Understanding these regulations is essential for all property owners in City Centre, Clearbrook, and all Abbotsford neighborhoods.

Key Bylaw Requirements

  • Sidewalk Clearing: Must be cleared within 24 hours of snow stopping or ice forming
  • Snow Placement: Snow must be placed on your property only, not on street or neighbor's property
  • Seasonal Duration: Requirements apply whenever snow or ice is present
  • Accessibility: Clear pathways must be safe for pedestrians and wheelchair users
  • Street Parking: Do not block storm drains or city access with snow piles
  • Driveways: Clear snow from driveway approaches to street

Fines & Enforcement

Abbotsford's Good Neighbour Bylaw No. 1256-2003 allows the City to issue fines for uncleared sidewalks, but unlike some neighboring cities, Abbotsford does not publish a fixed fine schedule. Historically, enforcement has been complaint-driven rather than proactive — local reporting has noted that in some years only a single fine was issued citywide. That said, the bylaw is in force year-round whenever snow or ice is present, and repeat or serious violations (such as a fully blocked sidewalk creating a hazard) are more likely to draw enforcement action.

Enforcement & City Actions

If property owners fail to comply with bylaws, the City of Abbotsford may:

  • Issue violation notices with 24-hour compliance deadlines
  • Perform snow removal at owner's expense (recovery through city billing)
  • Levy ongoing fines for continued violations
  • Place liens on properties for unpaid snow removal costs
  • Report violations to property insurance companies
  • Take legal action for serious violations

Report Snow & Ice Issues to Abbotsford

Use Abbotsford's reporting system to report uncleared sidewalks, hazardous icy conditions, and blocked drains on public property. This helps keep neighborhoods safe and ensures code enforcement, especially in busy areas like City Centre and Clearbrook.

How to Report

Call Abbotsford Engineering Operations

Phone: 604-853-5485 (or 604-864-5552 after hours)
Hours: Available during business hours; emergency line 24/7
Cost: Free service for Abbotsford residents

Online Reporting

Report online through the City of Abbotsford website:

City of Abbotsford Website

What to Report

  • Uncleared sidewalks in front of homes or businesses
  • Hazardous icy conditions on public streets and walkways
  • Blocked or frozen storm drains
  • Snow piles blocking visibility or traffic flow
  • Inadequate city snow removal on priority routes
  • Dangerous ice on wheelchair accessible paths
  • Blocked transit stop access (bus routes)

Response & Enforcement

Abbotsford's bylaw enforcement team will:

  • Investigate reported violations within 24-48 hours (weather permitting)
  • Issue violation notices to non-compliant property owners
  • Allow 24 hours for property owners to clear snow
  • Perform city snow removal at owner's expense if non-compliance continues
  • Continue enforcement and fines for repeat violations

Abbotsford School Closures Due to Winter Weather

Winter weather can occasionally force school closures in Abbotsford. Here's how to stay informed about closures for Abbotsford's school district.

Abbotsford School District (SD 34)

Decision Time: Closure decisions are made by 6:30 a.m. on the day in question.
  • The public school district serving Abbotsford
  • Covers approximately 20,000 students
  • Serves all of Abbotsford including City Centre, Clearbrook, West Abbotsford, Historic Downtown

How to Get Notifications:

  • Check SD 34 website: www.abbyschools.ca
  • School closure page: SD 34 Closure Information
  • Enable ParentAlert text/email notifications
  • Check the district's Winter Weather page for updates
  • Check local news stations (CityNews, CTV, Global)
  • Call your child's school directly for confirmation

City Centre & Clearbrook Area Schools

Schools in City Centre and Clearbrook areas are served by Abbotsford School District (SD 34). These include elementary, middle, and secondary schools throughout the commercial and residential centers.

Winter Closure Preparedness Tips

  • Update emergency contact information with your school immediately
  • Sign up for ParentAlert and all notification systems
  • Don't assume schools are open (only closures/delays are announced)
  • Have a backup childcare plan ready
  • Check closure announcements by 7 a.m. on winter mornings
  • Monitor weather forecasts the night before
  • Verify with school website if uncertain

Abbotsford 10-Year Snow & Ice Climate Averages

Abbotsford experiences moderate winter weather, receiving more snow than coastal Vancouver but less than inland Surrey or elevated North Shore areas. Understanding typical conditions helps you prepare effectively for City Centre, Clearbrook, and all neighborhoods.

Annual Snow Statistics

Metric Average
Annual snowfall 45-65 cm (18-26 inches)
Snow days per year 12-18 days with measurable snow
Days with snow on ground 25-45 days with 1cm+ accumulation
Heaviest month January (3-4 snow days, 100-150mm)
Second heaviest February (2-3 snow days, 80-120mm)

Monthly Snow Breakdown

  • December: Variable, occasional light accumulation
  • January: Usually the snowiest month, though totals vary widely winter to winter
  • February: Often the heaviest month on record, averaging around 19cm
  • March: Transition month, melting typically occurs
  • April: Minimal snow (trace to 5mm)
  • May-November: Essentially no snow

Freezing & Ice Days

Common

Freezing temperatures are common through December-February

Abbotsford experiences freeze-thaw cycles, creating icy conditions even with light snow. This is particularly common in January and February. Temperature Patterns

  • Winter average: around 3°C in January (coldest month), with colder overnight lows than the coast
  • Freezing point days: more frequent than coastal Metro Vancouver due to valley cold-air pooling
  • Below -5°C: occurs most winters during cold snaps
  • Severe cold: Occasional events below -10°C during Arctic outflow conditions

Regional Variations Within Abbotsford

  • Historic Downtown: Slightly warmer, lower elevation
  • City Centre (central): Average conditions, moderate snow
  • Clearbrook (north): Slightly higher elevation, slightly more snow
  • Sumas Mountain: Variable depending on elevation
