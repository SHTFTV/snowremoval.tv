Look downhill from the pile

A snow pile is also a future source of water. Before choosing its location, trace where meltwater would travel across the site. A pile uphill from an entrance may repeatedly wet a cleared path. Mark nearby drains and ask the operator to keep their locations visible throughout the event.

Original PlowWow illustrative brand animation. Not a record of a customer job.

Keep access in the plan

Leave doors, hydrants, loading areas and accessible routes unobstructed. Check sightlines at driveways and pedestrian crossings. Snow storage should not create a new obstruction for people who cannot step over a windrow. Walk the proposed route after the first service to see how the arrangement works in practice.

Original PlowWow illustrative brand animation. Not a record of a customer job.

Prepare for a larger event

A small corner may fill early in the season. Agree a secondary storage area and ask how additional handling or hauling would be quoted. Do not assume removal from the property is included in a clearing contract. Document who can approve extra work so the operator does not have to guess during a storm.

Original PlowWow illustrative brand animation. Not a record of a customer job.

Inspect during the thaw

Look for standing water and the routes it crosses. Mark recurring trouble spots for a discussion with the property manager and provider. If drainage infrastructure appears blocked or defective, arrange an appropriate assessment. Changing an ice-control product will not correct an underlying drainage problem.
