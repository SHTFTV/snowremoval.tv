Choose a Snow Shovel for the Surface and the Task

Push or lift?

A broad pusher can move a shallow layer along a clear path. A smaller scoop makes more sense where snow must be lifted over a short obstacle. Neither choice removes the need to limit the load. Wet coastal snow can become heavy quickly; use small amounts and avoid trying to finish an entire walkway in one lift.

Protect the surface

Check the blade edge against the pavement and any manufacturer guidance. A sharp metal edge may catch raised joints or mark a sensitive finish. Inspect the route for loose pavers, mats and hidden steps before starting. Keep snow away from door thresholds rather than leaving a ridge that later freezes.

Original PlowWow illustrative brand animation. Not a record of a customer job.

Set a manageable working area

Clear in short sections with space for people to pass safely. Do not throw snow onto a neighbouring cleared route or back into the road. Keep your movements controlled and stop if the task exceeds your ability. Arrange professional help for heavy accumulation, difficult access or a large property.

Original PlowWow illustrative brand animation. Not a record of a customer job.

Store it ready for use

Keep shovels dry and close to the entrance, with the chosen ice-control supplies and a visible route plan. Replace cracked blades and loose handles before the next event. After clearing, inspect for remaining slush and water; a clean-looking path may refreeze. Review the walkway guide for the next inspection.
