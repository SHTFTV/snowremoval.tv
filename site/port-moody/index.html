Everything You Need to Know About Snow Removal in Port Moody

Port Moody, with a population of 33,500+, is one of the smaller cities in the Tri-Cities area. Tucked at the head of Burrard Inlet with hilly, waterfront-facing streets, Port Moody sees winter conditions that can ice up faster than flatter parts of Metro Vancouver. This comprehensive guide covers Port Moody's snow removal policies, service priorities, bylaws and penalties, how to report snow issues, school closure procedures, and climate data to help you prepare for winter.

Property Owner Responsibilities

Legal Requirement: Property owners must clear snow and ice from sidewalks bordering their property within 24 hours of a snowfall, with a same-day 10 a.m. deadline on Priority Pedestrian Routes, under Port Moody Bylaw No. 2426.
  • Clear all sidewalks adjacent to your property daily
  • Shovel snow onto your property, not the street or into traffic
  • Remove ice and apply sand or salt for traction
  • Clear storm drains and grates
  • Maintain clear pathways for pedestrians
  • Ensure wheelchair and mobility device access
  • Keep driveways clear to avoid blocking street access

Port Moody Snow Removal Bylaws & Penalties

The City of Port Moody enforces snow removal bylaws under Bylaw No. 2426. Understanding these regulations is essential for all property owners in Moody Centre, Newport Village, and all Port Moody neighbourhoods.

Key Bylaw Requirements

  • Sidewalk Clearing: Cleared within 24 hours of snowfall; by 10 a.m. daily on Priority Pedestrian Routes
  • Snow Placement: Snow must be placed on your property only, not on street or neighbor's property
  • Seasonal Duration: Requirements apply whenever snow or ice is present
  • Accessibility: Clear pathways must be safe for pedestrians and wheelchair users
  • Street Parking: Do not block storm drains or city access with snow piles
  • Driveways: Clear snow from driveway approaches to street

Fines & Penalties for Non-Compliance

Violation Fine Amount
Standard sidewalk violation $100
Priority Pedestrian Route violation (1st offense) $100
Priority Pedestrian Route violation (repeat) Up to $500
Blocked fire hydrant Minimum $100

Fines can be issued daily until the violation is cleared, and the City may complete the work itself and bill the property owner.

Enforcement & City Actions

If property owners fail to comply with bylaws, the City of Port Moody may:

  • Issue violation notices with 24-hour compliance deadlines
  • Perform snow removal at owner's expense (recovery through city billing)
  • Levy ongoing fines for continued violations
  • Place liens on properties for unpaid snow removal costs
  • Report violations to property insurance companies
  • Take legal action for serious violations

Report Snow & Ice Issues in Port Moody

Use Port Moody's reporting system to report uncleared sidewalks, hazardous icy conditions, and blocked drains on public property. This helps keep neighborhoods safe and ensures code enforcement, especially in busy areas like Moody Centre and Newport Village.

How to Report

Call Port Moody

Phone: 604-469-4574
Hours: Operations line available 24/7 for urgent hazards; online form available anytime
Cost: Free service for Port Moody residents

Online Reporting

Report online through the City of Port Moody website:

City of Port Moody Website

What to Report

  • Uncleared sidewalks in front of homes or businesses
  • Hazardous icy conditions on public streets and walkways
  • Blocked or frozen storm drains
  • Snow piles blocking visibility or traffic flow
  • Inadequate city snow removal on priority routes
  • Dangerous ice on wheelchair accessible paths
  • Blocked transit stop access (SkyTrain, bus routes)

Response & Enforcement

Port Moody's bylaw enforcement team will:

  • Investigate reported violations, weather and workload permitting
  • Issue violation notices to non-compliant property owners
  • Give property owners a reasonable opportunity to clear snow before further action
  • Contact Bylaw Enforcement directly (604-469-4697) for questions about a specific case
  • Apply fines under Bylaw No. 2426 for confirmed, uncorrected violations

Port Moody School Closures Due to Winter Weather

Winter weather can occasionally force school closures in Port Moody. Here's how to stay informed about closures for Port Moody's school district.

Port Moody School District (SD 43)

Decision Time: Closure decisions are made by 6:30 a.m. on the day in question.
  • The school district serving Port Moody and the Tri-Cities
  • Covers Port Moody along with Coquitlam, Port Coquitlam, Anmore, and Belcarra (approximately 35,000 students district-wide)
  • Serves all of Port Moody including Moody Centre, Newport Village, Heritage Mountain, Glenayre

How to Get Notifications:

  • Check SD 43 website: www.sd43.bc.ca
  • School closure page: SD 43 Closure Information
  • Enable ParentAlert text/email notifications
  • Check the district's Emergency Information page for updates
  • Check local news stations (CityNews, CTV, Global)
  • Call your child's school directly for confirmation

Moody Centre & Newport Village Area Schools

Schools in Moody Centre and Newport Village areas are served by Port Moody School District (SD 43). These include elementary, middle, and secondary schools throughout the commercial and residential centers.

Winter Closure Preparedness Tips

  • Update emergency contact information with your school immediately
  • Sign up for ParentAlert and all notification systems
  • Don't assume schools are open (only closures/delays are announced)
  • Have a backup childcare plan ready
  • Check closure announcements by 7 a.m. on winter mornings
  • Monitor weather forecasts the night before
  • Verify with school website if uncertain

Port Moody Snow & Ice Climate Averages

Port Moody experiences moderate coastal winter weather, broadly similar to neighbouring Burnaby and Vancouver, generally with less snow than inland valley communities like Surrey or Langley and less than the higher-elevation North Shore. Understanding typical conditions helps you prepare effectively for Glenayre, Moody Centre, Newport Village, College Park, and all neighbourhoods.

Annual Snow Statistics

Metric Average
Annual snowfall approximately 25-35 cm (10-14 inches) in a typical winter
Snow days per year a handful of days most winters, concentrated in December and January
Days with snow on ground varies by winter; typically brief, with snow melting within a few days at low elevation
Heaviest month January (average snowfall around 12cm/4.7in)
Second heaviest December (average snowfall around 10cm/3.8in)

Monthly Snow Breakdown

  • November: Snow season begins, light accumulation (around 2.5cm/1in average)
  • December: Snow becomes more likely (around 10cm/3.8in average)
  • January: Typically the snowiest month (around 12cm/4.7in average)
  • February: Snow tapering off (around 7cm/2.7in average)
  • March: Transition month, light snow possible (around 2cm/0.7in average)
  • April-October: Essentially no snow

Freezing & Ice Days

Common

Freezing temperatures are common through December and January

Port Moody experiences freeze-thaw cycles, creating icy conditions even with light snow, particularly overnight in December and January. Its position along Burrard Inlet keeps temperatures slightly milder than inland Tri-Cities neighbours, but hillier neighbourhoods away from the water can see colder overnight lows. For detailed winter weather analysis for Port Moody, visit PlowWow's Port Moody snow removal guide.

Temperature Patterns

  • Winter average: around 0°C overnight / 4-5°C daytime in December and January (coldest months)
  • Freezing point days: common overnight through December and January, less frequent by day
  • Below -5°C: occasional, mainly during Arctic outflow events
  • Severe cold: rare, but possible during multi-day outflow events

Regional Variations Within Port Moody

  • Glenayre: Slightly warmer, lower elevation
  • Moody Centre (central): Average conditions, moderate snow
  • Newport Village (north): Slightly higher elevation, slightly more snow
  • College Park: Variable depending on elevation

Comparison to Nearby Cities

Port Moody's location provides moderate winter weather compared to other Lower Mainland communities:

  • vs. Vancouver/Burnaby: Broadly similar, coastal-influenced winters
  • vs. Coquitlam/Port Coquitlam: Comparable, though inland Tri-Cities areas away from the inlet can see slightly colder overnight lows
  • vs. North Vancouver/West Vancouver: Generally less snow, since the North Shore mountains sit at higher elevation
  • vs. Surrey/Langley: Generally similar to slightly less, as inland valley locations can trend snowier
